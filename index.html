      Your two votes

      Under MMP you get two votes, and you can give them to different parties. Watch the video or read the summary below.

      Party vote

      For a party. Decides how many of Parliament's 120 seats each party gets, roughly in proportion to its share.

      Electorate vote

      For your local MP. The candidate with the most votes in your electorate wins. There are 71 electorates: 64 general and 7 Māori.

      How votes become seats
      • The threshold: a party needs at least 5% of the party vote, or to win an electorate, to get seats in Parliament.
      • Electorate MPs are the 71 electorate winners. List MPs fill the remaining seats from each party's ranked list, so each party's total roughly matches its party vote.
      • Forming a government: one party rarely wins enough seats to govern alone. In a coalition, two or more parties form the government together. In a confidence and supply agreement, a party agrees to support the government on certain issues and votes in Parliament.

        Seat calculator

        A what-if tool, not a forecast. It starts from the 14-day poll average and uses the Electoral Commission's counting method to share out the 120 seats.

        Try it: move a slider and watch the seats change. A party below 5% still gets seats if it wins an electorate, so tick Wins electorate for any party you think will win one.

        How the seats are worked out
        • A party gets seats only if it wins at least 5% of the party vote or at least one electorate.
        • Votes for parties that don't qualify are left out, and the 120 seats are shared among the rest using the Sainte-Laguë formula: each party's vote is divided by 1, 3, 5, 7 and so on, and the 120 highest results win seats.
        • A party's electorate MPs count towards its share; list MPs fill the rest. If a party wins more electorates than its share, it keeps them and Parliament grows by those extra "overhang" seats.
        • To govern, a party or group of parties needs more than half of all seats: 61 of 120, or more if there are overhang seats.
        • This calculator rounds to what you enter. Real results use exact vote counts, so they can differ by a seat or two.
