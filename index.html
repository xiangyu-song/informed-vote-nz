7 November is election day, a Saturday.

    Your two votes

    New Zealand uses MMP. On the voting paper you have two separate votes, and you can give them to different parties or candidates.

    Party vote

    For a party. This decides how many of Parliament's 120 seats each party gets, roughly in proportion to its share of the party vote.

    Electorate vote

    For a candidate to be your local MP. The candidate with the most votes in your electorate wins the seat. There are 71 electorates in 2026: 64 general and 7 Māori electorates.

    How votes become seats

    • The threshold: a party needs at least 5% of the party vote, or to win an electorate, to get seats in Parliament.
    • Electorate MPs are the 71 electorate winners. List MPs fill the remaining seats from each party's ranked list, so that each party's total roughly matches its share of the party vote.
    • Forming a government: one party rarely wins enough seats to govern alone, so parties usually make agreements. In a coalition, two or more parties form the government together. In a confidence and supply agreement, a party agrees to support the government on certain issues and votes in Parliament.

    Find your electorate by address on vote.nz, the Electoral Commission's official site. The Commission also publishes the candidates standing in each electorate and every party's list there.

    Common questions

    Answers from the Electoral Commission's official site, vote.nz. Tap a question to open it. For anything else, call the Commission free on 0800 36 76 56.

    Do I need ID to vote?

    No. You don't need to take ID. Your EasyVote card makes voting faster, but if you don't have it or forget it, you can still vote. vote.nz: What to do

    Can I give my two votes to different parties?

    Yes. Your party vote and your electorate vote are separate: tick one party on the left side of the voting paper and one candidate on the right. You can also vote for only one of them. It's your choice. vote.nz: What to do

    Can I enrol or update my details on election day?

    No. You can't enrol or update your details after midnight on Sunday 25 October. If you're not enrolled by then, you can't vote in this election. vote.nz: Enrol by 25 October

    I've moved house. Can I still vote?

    Yes, but if you haven't updated your address you may need to make a special vote, and if your new address is in a different electorate, only your party vote will count. Update your address by midnight on 25 October to avoid this. vote.nz: Enrol by 25 October

    Can I vote anywhere, or only near home?

    You can vote at any voting place in New Zealand. If you vote away from your home electorate, you'll fill in a short "special declaration vote" form first. Some voting places open only before election day and some only on the day, and hours vary. vote.nz: Find a voting place

    What if I make a mistake on my voting paper?

    Take the spoilt voting paper back and ask for another one. vote.nz: What to do

    I can't get to a voting place. What can I do?

    Someone can collect your voting papers for you with a form you sign, and return them before voting closes on election day. If you're in a rest home or care home, voting is arranged with the home. If you're going into hospital, it's best to vote early. If none of these work, your electorate manager can help. vote.nz: Can't get to a voting place?

    I'm blind, have low vision or a disability. How can I vote?

    You can bring a support person (a friend, family member or electoral official) who can go behind the screen with you. You can also vote by phone with the telephone dictation service: register by noon on 7 November (even if you've used it before); phone voting runs from 21 October until 7pm on election day. vote.nz: Need help to vote?

    How do I vote from overseas?

    Overseas voting starts on Wednesday 21 October. The easiest way is to download your voting papers from vote.nz, fill them in and upload them by 7pm (NZ time) on election day. You may also be able to vote at an overseas voting place. vote.nz: Vote from overseas

    Can I post a photo or share my vote online?

    Please don't film or take photos in a voting place. From midnight before election day until 7pm on election day, you can't post or share anything likely to influence how people vote. vote.nz: What to expect at the voting place

    When will we know the results?

    Preliminary results are released from 7pm on election night at electionresults.govt.nz. Official results are declared on 27 November. vote.nz: Key dates
