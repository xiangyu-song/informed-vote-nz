    Your two votes

    New Zealand uses MMP. On the voting paper you have two separate votes, and you can give them to different parties or candidates.

    Party vote

    For a party. This decides how many of Parliament's 120 seats each party gets, roughly in proportion to its share of the party vote.

    Electorate vote

    For a candidate to be your local MP. The candidate with the most votes in your electorate wins the seat. There are 71 electorates in 2026: 64 general and 7 Māori electorates.

    How votes become seats

    • The threshold: a party needs at least 5% of the party vote, or to win an electorate, to get seats in Parliament.
    • Electorate MPs are the 71 electorate winners. List MPs fill the remaining seats from each party's ranked list, so that each party's total roughly matches its share of the party vote.
    • Forming a government: one party rarely wins enough seats to govern alone, so parties usually make agreements. In a coalition, two or more parties form the government together. In a confidence and supply agreement, a party agrees to support the government on certain issues and votes in Parliament.

    Find your electorate by address on vote.nz, the Electoral Commission's official site. The Commission also publishes the candidates standing in each electorate and every party's list there.
